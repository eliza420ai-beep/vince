## Hyperlquid — quick take

Jeff Yan (founder/CEO) returns to explain why Hyperlquid’s mission is to put finance on‑chain before machine intelligence redefines how value moves. The project runs an L1 with an on‑chain order book that, per the interview, has produced billions in volume and millions in daily revenue; a community token event created large, rapid wealth transfers but day‑to‑day operations didn’t change and the core team remains small and focused. Yan frames Hyperlquid as a set of neutral, permissionless financial primitives built to compose across many teams and products rather than a centralized exchange or discretionary corporate treasury. *"I hope Hyperlid becomes the internet for money."*

## What he says matters most (products, rules, design)

Yan disputes critiques that Hyperlquid runs discretionary buybacks: the protocol converts fees to the token and destroys them via on‑chain logic rather than through an off‑chain, manager‑controlled treasury — *"Hyperlid burns its protocol fees."* — an analogy he likens to Ethereum’s fee burn. Key technical pieces discussed are HyperEVM (an EVM-compatible execution layer that gives contracts a portal into native L1 primitives), HIP3 (a permissionless perpetuals framework that lets deployers create perps on many assets), outcome markets (fully collateralized markets for nonlinear payoffs such as options or binary bets), and on‑chain spot trading; these primitives are intended to be small, composable, and implemented natively so external builders can plug in. Yan emphasizes that many important integration details are different from other chains — integrations can be “invisible” to users once they’re done correctly — and that some early HIP3 deployments already captured meaningful market share in niche flows.

## Operations, culture, risk posture and strategy

Hyperlquid’s core organization is deliberately tiny (roughly 11 core team members) while the ecosystem is broad and developer‑driven; the protocol’s philosophy is to push non‑opinionated, resilient primitives on‑chain and let domain experts build around them rather than centralize everything. Yan repeatedly stresses integrity, fairness, and on‑chain transparency as design constraints that slow some growth choices but reduce systemic risk; the team tests hires and cultural fit through extended collaboration and grants veto authority to skeptical teammates. He describes practical results so far — e.g., HIPP3 markets gaining a multi‑percent share of some niche global price discovery (silver trade example) and recent on‑chain spot launches that led Hyperlquid to be a leading transparent venue — and frames the longer horizon as preparing finance to be programmable and accessible when AI systems increasingly transact value.
