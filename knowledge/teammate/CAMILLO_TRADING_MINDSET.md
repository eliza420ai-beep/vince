# Chris Camillo Trading Mindset (Social Arbitrage)

Reference for WTT and narrative trading. Chris Camillo (retail investor, ~77% annual compounded over 15 years, audited) uses **social arbitrage**: information edge from real-world and social behavior **before** Wall Street prices it in.

## Core Ideas

1. **Information imbalance**  
   The edge is the time between *you* noticing a meaningful behavioral/social change and when that info reaches the Street. "Once the information becomes universally known, it's fully reflected in the stock price." **It's all about being early.**

2. **Social + real-world observation**  
   Use TikTok, Twitter, Reddit, Instagram, and everyday life (shelf space, what people search, where they go). Not fundamentals or technicals first—**observable behavior** that implies demand or neglect before earnings or headlines.

3. **Meaningful trends only**  
   Not every trend. "Ones that have potential to be really meaningful … impacting the entire country or an entire subset of people." Skip small or bleeding-edge fads; focus on **big, durable behavioral shifts**.

4. **Thesis validity over price**  
   Don't judge the trade by P&L alone. Ask: **Is the thesis still true?** If the behavioral signal is intact, drawdowns can be noise. If the signal has reversed or the info is now universal, exit.

5. **Pure play**  
   Prefer the clearest expression of the thesis. "Took me a few hours to find one that was a **pure play** on beach resorts." Avoid conglomerates where one division can drown the story (e.g. Tim Hortons vs Burger King/Popeyes).

6. **Exit when info is in the price**  
   Exit gradually as the narrative becomes consensus and shows up in revenue/earnings. "Often I'm not fully exited until I see that information fully reflected in the company's revenue and earnings and as it's disclosed by the company itself."

7. **Know what you don't know**  
   His big loss (RBI) came from a division he had **no social or consumer data on** (Tim Hortons, Canada). Lesson: size down or skip when part of the story is a blind spot.

## For WTT

- **Thesis:** Frame as a **behavioral or social signal** that the market hasn't fully priced (e.g. "OpenAI named US gov AI provider" = institutional adoption signal; "CT + headlines all in on X" = consider fade). Prefer **pure play** tickers.
- **Invalidation:** Define exit as **thesis broken** or **info now universal** (e.g. "earnings show the trend; exit" or "headline is front-page; edge gone"), not only price levels.
- **Narrative:** Say *why* this is an information imbalance (what you see that the Street may not yet), and what would prove the thesis wrong or "priced in."
