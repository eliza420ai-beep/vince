## 1. "No Tilt" — https://ikigaistudio.substack.com/p/no-tilt

**Opening hook:**
> Most AI trading projects die the same way bad poker players go broke: not because they were wrong, but because they didn't know how to be right.

**Key traits:**
- Opens with a provocative comparison/analogy
- Direct, no fluff
- Uses concrete examples (poker, Kelly criterion, 3am sessions)
- Makes bold claims backed by framework
- Short paragraphs, high information density

**Tone:** Sharp, confident, practitioner-to-practitioner

## 2. "Ideas Matter" — https://ikigaistudio.substack.com/p/ideas-matter

**Opening hook:**
> A guy buys four Mac Studios and a Mac Mini. Puts them on his desk. Installs open-source models that match or beat the closed-source frontier. Builds a cluster that runs inference 24/7 — no API limits, no cloud dependency, no permission required.

**Key traits:**
- Opens with a vivid, specific scenario
- Builds to a thesis: "This is the most important inversion in the history of work"
- Contrast between old world vs new world
- Ends with conviction

**Tone:** Visionary, bold, builds narrative momentum

## 3. "The Stack" — https://ikigaistudio.substack.com/p/the-stack

**Opening hook:**
> A 23-year-old in Lagos can form a Wyoming LLC from his phone, tokenize equity in his company before lunch, issue a branded stablecoin by dinner, and fractionalize a piece of Miami real estate for his community by the time he goes to bed.

**Key traits:**
- Contrast-driven (Lagos vs Paris)
- Specific details (costs, timelines)
- Identifies a trend nobody is talking about
- Builds thesis from observation

**Tone:** Observational, contrarian, data-informed

## THE IKIGAI STYLE

**Core principles:**
1. **Hook first** — Lead with the most interesting take, not "Today I'll write about..."
2. **Short sentences** — One idea per sentence. No run-ons.
3. **Concrete > abstract** — Specific examples, not generic statements
4. **Conviction** — Say what you believe, not what "some people think"
5. **Contrarian when true** — Don't seek conflict, but don't shy from it
6. **No apologizing** — No "I might be wrong" or "This is just my opinion"
7. **End with clarity** — One clear takeaway, not "Thanks for reading"
8. **No lists** — Unless genuinely useful, avoid bullet points
9. **Earn the reader's time** — Every paragraph should add something

**What to avoid:**
- Filler ("In today's landscape...")
- Hedging ("Perhaps...", "It could be argued...")
- 101 explanations (reader is smart)
- "AI slop" language (leverage, utilize, synergy, cutting-edge, game-changer)
- Long intros or conclusions
