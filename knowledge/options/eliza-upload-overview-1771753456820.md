# ### Overview

> **Knowledge base note:** Numbers and metrics here are illustrative from the source; use for methodologies and frameworks, not as current data. For live data use VINCE.

## Content

### Overview
Jamie Coutts interviews Charles Edwards about the growing market concern that quantum computing poses an existential risk to Bitcoin. Edwards argues the market is already pricing that risk, which helps explain Bitcoin's underperformance versus gold in 2025 despite broad liquidity tailwinds. He frames the problem as both technical (upgrading cryptography) and social (reaching consensus across a decentralized network).

### Quantum threat, timelines and technical hurdles
Edwards places a nonzero risk of quantum machines breaking Bitcoin’s cryptography within 2–3 years and a more likely risk horizon of 5–8 years, driven by public estimates and government signals. Centralized institutions can patch and upgrade more quickly (2FA, coordinated rollouts, insurance), while Bitcoin’s decentralization makes a timely protocol upgrade much harder. The main technical debate centers on quantum-resistant signature schemes: many strong options are much larger, which would reduce transactions per block unless block parameters change; BIP 360 is a current stepping stone but removed the signature swap, so more steps remain. *"Bitcoin is the easiest target and the biggest honeypot."*

### The exposed-public-key (Satoshi) problem
About 20–30% of Bitcoin supply has public keys exposed on-chain (addresses that can’t be passively migrated because the owner must sign), and many of those coins appear lost or inaccessible. Edwards warns those coins could be stolen by a quantum-capable actor and then market-dumped, causing catastrophic loss of trust and a large supply shock. One proposed mitigation: require migration to quantum-proof wallets by a deadline, after which non-migrated, exposed coins would be rendered non-transactable — a tradeoff that conflicts with property-rights principles and is politically fraught. *"once the money's gone, it's gone forever, pretty much."*

### Market signals, mining, treasuries and takeaways
Edwards says markets are discounting the quantum risk (his fund is currently flat on Bitcoin until clearer roadmaps emerge). Hash rate has fallen notably since November — a large drawdown comparable only to the China-era drop — which changes miner economics and the price-support heuristics he tracks (production-cost and energy-value bands; a cited deep value range was roughly $53–66k). He flags structural limits in the ~200 public “Bitcoin treasury” companies (MNAV dilution, growing use of debt in ~20–30% of firms) and describes quantum equities as a diversified hedge, not a direct profit-from-hacking thesis. He also notes his team uses LLMs and ML heavily for research and that broader macro liquidity (money supply growth) is supportive for risk assets and hard assets.
